## **Spectralities of Remote Warfare: Synthesizing Feminist IR, Hauntology, and the Re-articulation of Military Masculinities** {#spectralities-of-remote-warfare}

### 1. Introduction: Epistemological Foundations of Gender and Military Technology

Analyzing military technologies through a gendered lens constitutes a critical epistemological imperative for International Relations (IR) and critical war studies. Moving decisively beyond technological determinism—a flawed paradigm that treats weapons platforms as autonomous, politically neutral drivers of strategic outcomes—a gendered critique reveals war as a profoundly human, embodied, and discursively constructed social phenomenon. Military technologies do not emerge or operate within a structural vacuum; rather, they are thoroughly embedded within socio-political matrices and imbued with normative cultural assumptions regarding power, human agency, and legitimate state violence. Interrogating war technologies through a feminist security lens reveals how weaponry both reflects and actively reconstitutes broader hierarchical structures of inequality, somatic vulnerability, and discursive regimes of martial force.

Traditional security studies approaches to military technology—spanning historical analyses of weaponry to contemporary paradigms such as Revolutions in Military Affairs (RMA)—have historically privileged material capabilities, strategic utility, and state-centric force projection. Within these conventional frameworks, technological innovation is evaluated predominantly through metrics of tactical efficiency, lethality, and operational optimization, while the human-technology nexus is reduced to rationalized command-and-control dynamics. Conversely, Feminist Security Studies (FSS) interventions demonstrate that gender and warfare are co-constituted. Feminist theorists deconstruct the traditional obfuscation of human embodiment, demonstrating that gender functions not as an isolated variable or individual attribute, but as a primary structural organizing principle that delineates power, political agency, and the monopoly on violence.

Feminist scholarship systematically dismantles the myth of technological neutrality by illuminating how weapons platforms and strategic doctrines are saturated with phallic, sexualized, and gendered rhetoric. Groundbreaking interventions, such as Carol Cohn’s seminal critique of nuclear defense intellectuals, exposed how "techno-strategic language" uses sexualized euphemisms and gendered tropes to insulate defense strategists from the visceral, somatic realities of mass destruction. In this discourse, weapons platforms are routinely conceptualized as "penetrators" unleashing energy in an "orgasmic whump," while functional weapons are gendered as "boys" and duds as "girls." This phallic symbolism extends across military history, linking the penetrative thrust of swords, artillery, and firearms to masculine potency, political control, and compulsory heteronormative dominance.

The operational deployment of Remotely Piloted Aerial Systems (RPAS)—specifically Medium Altitude, Long Endurance (MALE) armed drones such as the MQ-9 Reaper—presents a profound disruption to these established theoretical frameworks. By decoupling lethal force projection from physical presence and somatic risk, remote warfare challenges traditional martial paradigms. This transformation prompts the core research question of this study: _How does the deployment of Remotely Piloted Aerial Systems, specifically armed drones like the MQ-9 Reaper, simultaneously destabilize and re-inscribe traditional gendered binaries and warrior archetypes?_ To answer this question, critical scholarship must move beyond totalizing frameworks and interrogate the intense scholarly debate surrounding the gendered implications of remote conflict.

### 2. Dualistic Paradigms in Drone Scholarship: Feminisation versus Hyper-masculinisation

Scholarly literature addressing the gendered impacts of remote warfare on military institutions and RPAS crews has largely bifurcated into two polar, dualistic paradigms. On one side, commentators assert that drone operations trigger a "feminisation" and martial degradation of the armed forces by insulating combatants from physical risk. On the other side, critical scholars contend that remote warfare represents an intensification of hegemonic masculinity through techno-fetishism, panoptic surveillance, and spatial dominance. However, these binary frameworks fail to capture the operational realities of RPAS crews, forcing complex, contradictory human experiences into rigid analytical dichotomies.

- **The Feminisation Thesis (Degradation, Moral Deskilling, and Emasculation):**
  - **Erosion of Somatic Risk and Chivalric Valor:** Rooted in classic martial traditions, this perspective posits that removing physical danger from the battlefield erodes the foundational pillar of hegemonic military masculinity. Operating from distant Ground Control Stations (GCS) without risking bodily harm, drone crews are framed as incapable of enacting chivalric valor, physical courage, or the heroic self-sacrifice traditionally required for true "warrior" status.
  - **Derogatory Discursive Framing:** Commentators such as Martin Van Creveld explicitly link the absence of bodily risk to military decline, arguing that the removal of physical combat emasculates the martial ethos and degrades military institutions. Outside feminist scholarship, popular and media commentary routinely mobilizes derogatory tropes such as "cubicle warriors," "armchair killers," "chair force," and "Dilbert goes to war," reducing remote operations to administrative, deskbound labor.
  - **Erosion of the Dead Warrior Ethos and Moral Deskilling:** Expanding this critique, Christopher Coker (_Waging War Without Warriors?_, _Warrior Geeks_) argues that technological automation strips combat of its existential meaning and destroys the traditional warrior ethos. Coker asserts that when risk-averse technologies replace somatic exposure, war loses its existential gravity, transforming martial duty into mere technical management. Complementing this, Shannon Vallor conceptualizes this shift as "moral deskilling," wherein automated and remote platforms remove the existential friction required to cultivate classical martial virtues.
- **The Hyper-masculinisation Thesis (Techno-Fetishism and Panoptic Domination):**
  - **Emotional Detachment and Calculated Rationality:** Counter-arguments advanced by feminist critical scholars (including Sumita Kunashakaran, Caroline Holmqvist, and Lauren Bayard de Volo) assert that remote warfare intensifies hegemonic military masculinity. Physical distance is argued to cultivate emotional detachment and stoicism—traits historically coded as hyper-masculine—enabling operators to execute lethal force with cold, calculated rationality.
  - **The Penetrative "Drone Stare":** The persistent surveillance capability of RPAS asserts a panoptic, sovereign power over targeted populations. Scholars emphasize that the uninvited penetration of foreign airspace by armed platforms represents a paternalistic, hyper-masculine assertion of dominance over feminized, submissive territories and bodies on the ground.
  - **Cyborgian Integration and Techno-Mastery:** Building on Cristina Masters' analysis of the "cyborg soldier," this position highlights how technical proficiency, data processing, and physical integration with advanced machinery yield a techno-fetishized male dominance. Mastery over complex technology replaces raw physical strength as the supreme marker of military potency.

When treated as mutually exclusive, both theoretical positions operate within a totalizing binary logic. The feminisation thesis oversimplifies remote warfare by reducing it to automated button-pushing, completely ignoring the intense psychological strain, moral injury, and emotional trauma experienced by crews. Conversely, the hyper-masculinisation thesis risks overlooking the acute disempowerment, pervasive institutional surveillance, and unexpected emotional intimacy that operators experience during long-term target observation. By forcing RPAS operational realities into an "either/or" formula (either feminized desk-workers OR hyper-masculine cyborgs), contemporary scholarship fails to capture how these contradictory dynamics operate simultaneously. Overcoming this theoretical impasse requires a hauntological framework capable of embracing non-binary spectral complexities.

### 3. Methodology and Framework: Derridean Hauntology and the Ghost Hunt

To escape totalizing binary categories, critical security studies and feminist IR turn to the theoretical framework of Hauntology and the methodology of the "ghost hunt." Originating in Jacques Derrida’s _Specters of Marx_ and further developed by Avery Gordon and Jessica Auchter, Hauntology interrogates the "spectral"—that which is neither fully present nor absent, neither living nor dead, neither purely visible nor entirely hidden. Hauntology replaces classical _ontologie_ (the philosophy of self-contained, present being) with _hauntologie_, prioritizing the persistent influence of historical traces, unresolved violences, and structural absences. In this context, technology operates as a Derridean _pharmakon_—simultaneously functioning as a remedy for somatic risk and a poison that degrades traditional martial identity through disembodiment and psychological trauma.

Rather than treating the "ghost" as a paranormal entity, this methodology approaches the specter as a conceptual metaphor and deconstructive logic operating through _différance_—the constant deferral and spectral lingering of meaning. The ghost hunt provides a critical vocabulary to capture unarticulated structural power, unearthing "sensuous" and emotional knowledges that traditional positivist methodologies discard.

Building upon Derridean deconstruction, Avery Gordon's sociological imagination, and Jessica Auchter's IR interventions, **Lindsay C. Clark (2019)** synthesized this spectral approach into a unified four-pillar hauntological model specifically designed to interrogate remote warfare:

#### **Clark's Four Pillars of the Haunting Framework**

1. **Complex Personhood:** Subjects exist within convoluted, contradictory social, psychological, cultural, and historical contexts that simultaneously empower and disempower them. RPAS operators cannot be reduced to flat, monolithic archetypes (e.g., pure heroic warriors vs. cold-blooded cubicle killers). Their lived experience encompasses conflicting identities—as lethal combatants, techno-bureaucratic workers, parents, and partners—that exceed rigid masculine/feminine categorizations.
2. **In/(hyper)visibility:** This pillar articulates the structural paradox of subject positions defined by simultaneous omniscience and intense scrutiny. While drone crews exercise an empowered, panoptic "God's-eye view" over targets on the ground (hyper-visibility of the Other), they simultaneously operate within an environment characterized by pervasive institutional surveillance, legal oversight, and societal obscurity (hyper-visibility and invisibility of the self).
3. **Disturbed Temporality:** Haunting disrupts linear chronologies, causing past, present, and future to collapse into one another. In remote warfare, persistent surveillance requires operators to monitor targets over days, weeks, or months, entangling real-time observation with memory. Furthermore, the immediate daily transition between executing lethal strikes and returning to domestic home life collapses the spatial and temporal boundaries that historically separated the war front from the home front.
4. **Power:** Power operates not merely through centralized, physical coercion, but through invisible, intricate (cob)webs of structural, discursive, and institutional forces. These webs discipline subjects, enforce gendered norms, dictate legitimate violence, and produce liminal zones of disempowerment even within sophisticated military command structures.

By operationalizing Clark's four-pillar framework, critical researchers can trace how structural power haunts remote warfare. Rather than forcing empirical data into neat explanatory boxes, this methodology welcomes spectral ambiguities, providing an analytical bridge to examine the lived operational realities of Royal Air Force (RAF) Reaper crews.

### 4. Empirical Case Study: Operational Realities of British RAF Reaper Crews

Interrogating the operational realities of British Royal Air Force (RAF) crews operating the MQ-9 Reaper (specifically 39 Squadron and 13 Squadron operating out of RAF Waddington) provides crucial empirical grounding distinct from US targeted killing narratives. Public and academic discourse is heavily dominated by US CIA covert signature strikes in non-battlefield locations (e.g., Pakistan, Yemen, Somalia). In contrast, RAF Reaper operations emerged out of Urgent Operational Requirement (UOR) lineages to support integrated airpower campaigns within defined Rules of Engagement (ROE). Operating three-person crews (pilot, sensor operator, and intelligence analyst), RAF Reapers primarily perform Intelligence, Surveillance, Targeting, and Reconnaissance (ISTAR) and Close Air Support (CAS) for allied ground troops in active conflict zones such as Afghanistan, Iraq, and Syria. The UK experience offers a unique lens for analyzing how remote warfare destabilizes traditional martial identities.

The lived experiences of British RAF Reaper crews are defined by three primary operational paradoxes:

1. **Spatial and Temporal Disruption (Home Front vs. War Front):** Unlike traditionally deployed personnel who physically transition to overseas battlefields, RAF Reaper crews commute daily to Ground Control Stations (GCS) at RAF Waddington. Operators experience severe psychological and gendered friction as they cycle continuously between domestic domesticity and lethal combat. Testimonies from operators emphasize this jarring disruption: crews make school lunches, kiss spouses goodbye, and debate whether a daughter should wear a **"blue tutu or a pink tutu"** (Black, 2013; Rogoway, 2015), before walking through an aluminum door into a GCS cockpit to launch Hellfire missiles in Iraq or Syria. This routine boundary-crossing eliminates the traditional martial rite of passage of "going away to war" and deprives crews of post-combat decompression with peers, collapsing the spatial segregation that historically separated the feminized domestic hearth from the hyper-masculine battlefield.
2. **The Paradox of Surveillance and Intimacy (The "Drone Stare"):** While the persistent surveillance of the Reaper is discursively framed as an objective, hyper-masculine "God's-eye view," the operational practice of sensor operation generates unexpected emotional proximity. Crews perform prolonged "pattern of life" surveillance, observing targets over weeks in high-definition clarity—watching them play with their children, interact with families, and conduct daily routines. This prolonged gaze summons the **Ghost of Omniscience**, where the illusion of divine, omnipotent mastery is haunted by human vulnerability. When a strike is executed, operators observe the graphic aftermath in real-time. Far from fostering cold detachment, this intense gaze summons the **Ghost of the Watchman**, where lingering observation creates a profound, unsettling intimacy that induces severe moral strain, combat stress, and unique forms of psychological trauma.
3. **Hyper-Regulation and Institutional Gazes:** Despite wielding immense destructive capability, RAF operators experience extreme institutional regulation. Operator testimonies (such as 'Dan') explicitly describe RAF Reapers as **"the most supervised platform bar none."** Every operational decision, video feed, and cockpit transmission is recorded, scrutinized, and evaluated in real-time by military lawyers, senior commanders, and intelligence staff. This pervasive legal and procedural oversight severely curtails the traditional, romanticized autonomy of the military pilot. Operators navigate a paradoxical state of empowered technological dominance over ground targets alongside systemic disempowerment under the panoptic gaze of institutional oversight.

These empirical paradoxes illustrate that the lived realities of RPAS crews cannot be reduced to simple claims of either feminisation or hyper-masculinisation. Resolving these theoretical contradictions requires integrating hauntology with queer theory to conceptualize "both/and" operational subjectivities.

### 5. Queering the Warrior Myth: Conceptualizing "Both/And" Spectral Logics

To resolve the theoretical impasse in feminist drone literature, Clark's hauntological framework is synthesized with Cynthia Weber’s formulation of "queer logic(s)." In critical IR theory, queer logics deconstruct normative, heteronormative "either/or" binaries (such as masculine/feminine, combatant/non-combatant, home/front, presence/absence). Rather than forcing complex phenomena into rigid, mutually exclusive categories, queer logics open up a "both/and" (and `and/or`) analytical space. Applying this perspective demonstrates that remote warfare platforms do not simply feminize _or_ masculinize crews; rather, they _simultaneously destabilize and re-inscribe_ traditional gendered discourses and martial archetypes.

Central to this analytical synthesis is the deconstruction of the **Ghost of the Warrior**. The historical warrior archetype—built upon physical risk, stoic detachment, heroic sacrifice, and somatic potency—functions not as an empirical reality, but as a translucent, performative trope. This spectral ideal continuously haunts contemporary RPAS crews. When measured against this ghost, drone operators appear perpetually deficient because their operational environment lacks bodily danger, summoning the derogatory **Ghost of Cowardice**. However, a "both/and" logic reveals that the ghost of the warrior itself is composed of contradictory layers: a duty to kill alongside a duty to die; extreme technological mastery alongside physical risk-aversion; absolute local authority alongside strict institutional obedience. Remote operations re-articulate military masculinity by continuously interweaving these opposing dynamics.

As mapped in the synthesis matrix above, queer logics resolve the theoretical stalemate in drone scholarship. Remote warfare does not produce an absolute transition toward either military degradation or hyper-masculine dominance. Instead, RPAS operations exist within a spectral, liminal space where masculine and feminine, disembodied and intimate, empowered and regulated performative traits co-exist and continuously re-articulate one another.

### 6. Conclusion: Implications for Feminist International Relations and Critical Security Studies

Synthesizing Derridean hauntology, Clark's four-pillar framework, queer logics, and feminist security studies provides a robust theoretical model for analyzing the evolving landscape of remote warfare. Moving beyond reductionist debates that frame technological innovations as either purely feminizing or hyper-masculinizing forces, this approach reveals how military technologies interact with deeply sedimented gendered discourses. By centering the spectral, non-linear, and contradictory dimensions of RPAS operations, critical scholars can evaluate how modern weapons systems destabilize foundational IR binaries while simultaneously re-inscribing state power and military masculinities.

1. **Destabilization of Foundational IR Binaries:** Remote warfare collapses fundamental conceptual dichotomies that have historically structured International Relations and security studies, including front/back, combatant/non-combatant, protector/protected, presence/absence, and masculine/feminine.
2. **Re-articulation of Military Masculinities:** Technological proficiency, data fusion, and remote lethal authority replace physical somatic risk as primary sites for performing military masculinity, proving that martial gender norms are fluid, adaptive, and discursively re-articulated.
3. **Methodological Value of Lindsay C. Clark’s Haunting Framework:** Clark’s four pillars—complex personhood, in/(hyper)visibility, disturbed temporality, and power—provide a precise methodology for capturing marginalized knowledges, operational trauma, and structural paradoxes without forcing empirical data into totalizing categories.
4. **Distinctiveness of the UK Operational Context:** Empirical analysis of RAF Reaper crews (39 and 13 Squadrons) underscores the necessity of examining specific national, legal, and operational frameworks, counteracting the academic tendency to treat all RPAS operations as synonymous with covert US CIA targeted killing campaigns.
5. **Future Research Horizons:** The synthesis of hauntology and queer logics offers an analytical template that can be extended to investigate emerging military technologies, including lethal autonomous weapons systems (LAWS), algorithmic targeting AI, automated decision-support networks, and offensive cyber warfare.

Understanding the complex co-constitution of gender, human agency, and military technology requires abandoning totalizing, binary frameworks in favor of nuanced, non-binary analytical models capable of capturing the spectral, human realities of contemporary warfare.

## **Unmasking the Ghosts in the Machine: A Student's Guide to Hauntology and Gender in Drone Warfare** {#hauntology-in-drone-warfare}

### 1. Theoretical Foundations: What is Hauntology and Why Study Ghosts in War?

In the traditional study of modern warfare, conventional security approaches focus almost exclusively on quantifiable metrics: missile payloads, kill ratios, legal statutes, and troop numbers. While these traditional lenses provide strategic data, they frequently fail to capture the nuanced, contradictory, and spectral realities of modern combatants. To address these analytical blind spots, scholars in **critical security studies** have adopted **hauntology**—an analytical framework embedded within the broader **spectral turn** across social science and feminist international relations.

Pioneered in social theory by figures such as Jacques Derrida, Avery Gordon, and Jessica Auchter, **hauntology** is not an investigation into parapsychology, folklore, or the occult. Rather, it operates as a "deconstructive logic" and vocabulary using "ghosts" as a **conceptual metaphor**. Ghosts represent the enduring traces of history, unsaid realities, suppressed institutional anxieties, and non-linear power dynamics that continue to shape contemporary security practices.

> "Haunting is an encounter in which you touch the ghost or the ghostly matter of things: the ambiguities, the complexities of power and personhood, the violence and the hope, the looming and receding actualities, the shadows of ourselves and our society." — Avery Gordon (_Ghostly Matters: Haunting and the Sociological Imagination_, 2008)

Why is this framework essential for security studies students? Utilizing hauntology allows us to move beyond rigid, totalizing binaries—such as presence/absence, visible/invisible, or masculine/feminine—that traditionally dominate military scholarship. By applying this lens, scholars can examine the human realities of British Royal Air Force (RAF) Reaper drone crews operating remotely piloted aircraft (RPA) over conflict zones in Iraq, Syria, and Afghanistan from Ground Control Stations (GCS) located thousands of miles away.

Crucially, a hauntological approach requires students to account for vital context-specific operational nuances. For example, British RAF Reaper operations (conducted by 13 and 39 Squadrons) operate under formal military Rules of Engagement (ROE) within recognized operational theatres as part of a conventional airpower toolkit. This stands in stark contrast to covert United States Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) targeted killing campaigns involving "signature strikes" in non-declared warzones such as Pakistan, Yemen, and Somalia.

Yet, despite operating under standard airpower ROE, British RAF crews are continuously haunted by public disquiet and moral disquiet generated by US covert actions. The public and political controversies surrounding remote targeted killing bleed across international borders, creating a spectral web of disquiet that envelops British operators. Hauntology illuminates how these historical, political, and institutional ghosts shape the lived experiences and identity formations of military personnel.

Understanding this theoretical bedrock allows us to examine the first core pillar of hauntology: the complex personhood of the operators behind the screens.

### 2. Theoretical Pillar 1: Complex Personhood – Beyond the "Cubicle Warrior" Myth

At the core of Avery Gordon’s hauntological framework is the principle of **complex personhood**. This concept posits that "life is complicated" and that all human subjects—including military operators—exist as multifaceted beings enmeshed in convoluted, contradictory social, cultural, and historical structures. They are never merely static abstractions, passive instruments of military power, or flat caricatures.

Public commentary and popular media depictions frequently reduce British Reaper crews to flat, derogatory tropes. Critics across the political spectrum often label them with dismissive terms like "cubicle warriors," "chair force," "button pushers," "armchair killers," or "warrior geeks." These flat stereotypes imply that operating a remotely piloted aircraft from a distance is a riskless, mechanical office job that degrades traditional military virtue and erodes martial honor.

A hauntological analysis rejects these flat oversimplifications by uncovering the complex personhood of the three distinct roles that comprise a British Reaper crew operating within the Ground Control Station:

- **The Pilot:** An officer tasked with the physical flight control of the aircraft, navigational maneuvering, air traffic integration, and the final command authorization for kinetic weapon releases.
- **The Sensor Operator:** An aircrew member responsible for manipulating advanced optical sensors, infrared camera suites, and laser designation systems to maintain persistent surveillance over targets on the ground.
- **The Intelligence Analyst:** A specialist who interprets real-time data feeds, tracks long-term "patterns of life," cross-references multi-source intelligence, and calculates collateral damage estimates to inform tactical decisions.

Far from being detached video-game players or unfeeling administrative bureaucrats, these individuals navigate intense, high-stakes decisions regarding life and death while simultaneously remaining embedded in everyday civilian environments. They are parents, spouses, dog owners, car enthusiasts, drinkers, and teetotalers.

Recognizing the internal, multifaceted identities of these operators provides the necessary foundation for examining the unique visual environment in which they work—where watching and being watched create distinct spectral paradoxes.

### 3. Theoretical Pillar 2: In/(Hyper)visibility – Watching, Being Watched, and Spectral Surveillance

Remote drone operations fundamentally disrupt traditional military understandings of visibility, surveillance, and the visual "gaze." Under a hauntological lens, visibility is not a simple state of being seen or unseen; it operates as a double-edged, spectral paradox where hyper-visibility and complete invisibility exist simultaneously.

This surveillance paradox unfolds across two distinct operational dimensions:

1. **The External Gaze (Hyper-Visible Target / Invisible Operator):** Through **persistent surveillance**, the Reaper crew commands a continuous, high-definition "god's eye view" over subjects on the ground. This prolonged visual access—frequently termed **the drone stare**—establishes an asymmetrical position of power. The crew operates from an empowered, masculine-coded vantage point, watching targets for days or weeks across **liminal security-scapes**. Conversely, the target on the ground is rendered hyper-visible, exposed, and systematically feminized or subjugated beneath an unseen, omniscient optical force.
2. **The Internal Gaze (Invisible Public / Hyper-Visible Crew):** Externally, the Reaper crew operates shrouded in military secrecy, shielded from public view and physical battlefield threats. However, internally within the military apparatus, the crew experiences continuous institutional scrutiny. As one RAF crew member ("Dan") noted, the Reaper is "the most supervised platform bar none."

This dual architecture of surveillance creates a profound paradox regarding how power and gender are performed:

- **The Empowered Watcher (Masculine Coding):** The crew exercises total technological dominance and optical clarity over the battlefield, remaining physically invulnerable and emotionally detached while wielding lethal power.
- **The Supervised Watcher (Feminized Coding):** Every action, sensor focus, cockpit voice recording, and weapon release decision made by the crew is monitored, recorded, and evaluated in real time by senior commanders, legal officers, and intelligence oversight feeds. This relentless internal surveillance places the crew in a regulated, disempowered, and feminized posture of institutional subjugation.

The visual mechanics of this continuous surveillance feed directly into a profound disorientation of space and time for operating crews.

### 4. Theoretical Pillar 3: Disturbed Temporality – Commuting to War and Blurring the Home Hearth

Traditional warfare historically required a distinct physical and temporal separation: soldiers "went away" to war, crossing physical geographic borders to enter a bounded, distant combat theatre. This deployment provided a clear spatial boundary between the war zone and domestic life, followed by a formal period of transit and military "decompression" when returning home. Remote drone warfare dismantles this binary structure, generating severe **disturbed temporality** and spatial disorientation.

This spatial collapse is vividly illustrated by the operational environment of British Reaper crews stationed at RAF Waddington in Lincolnshire, England. At RAF Waddington, the Ground Control Stations—housed inside windowless, climate-controlled container units—sit directly adjacent to civilian base housing and children's playparks. An operator's daily routine involves dropping a child off at school, driving through routine civilian traffic, passing through an aluminum GCS door to launch lethal Hellfire strikes in Iraq or Syria, and then stepping back out into the Lincolnshire countryside to drive home for family dinner.

"Every decision you made was either … somebody living, saving somebody or somebody dying. ## **Case Study: Comparative Operational Contexts of British and American Armed Drone Warfare** {#case-study}

### 1. Introduction to Remotely Piloted Aircraft (RPA) in Modern Warfare

#### 1.1 Technical Profile of the MQ-9 Reaper

The General Atomics MQ-9 Reaper occupies a central position in contemporary military aviation, categorized doctrine-wide as a **Medium Altitude, Long Endurance (MALE)** remotely piloted aircraft. Originally developed for intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance (ISR) missions, the platform was systematically adapted to deliver precision-guided munitions directly within complex combat operational environments.

The technical specifications and tactical loadout capabilities of the MQ-9 Reaper include:

- **Airframe Dimensions:** A wingspan of 20.12 meters (66 feet), a height of 12.5 feet, and an overall fuselage length of 36 feet.
- **Armament Payload:** The capacity to carry high-precision strike munitions, specifically AGM-114 Hellfire air-to-ground missiles and GBU-12 500lb laser-guided bombs.
- **Operational Endurance:** Extended loiter time engineered for persistent intelligence, surveillance, targeting, and reconnaissance (ISTAR) coverage over designated target areas.

In defense and strategic studies, institutional language is heavily guarded. Military organizations explicitly reject the popular civilian term "drone," mandating instead the formal designations **Remotely Piloted Aircraft (RPA)** or **Remotely Piloted Air System (RPAS)**. This terminological precision is not merely cosmetic; it deliberately underscores that these platforms are not autonomous, unguided automatons. Rather, they are continuously controlled in real-time by fully qualified human crews operating from Ground Control Stations (GCS).

#### 1.2 Comparative Strategic Trajectories

The strategic integration of armed RPAs into Western defense structures followed markedly distinct trajectories in the United Kingdom and the United States, reflecting fundamentally divergent operational philosophies and institutional mandates.

##### The Royal Air Force (RAF) Context

The United Kingdom initially procured a fleet of six Reapers under Urgent Operational Requirements (UOR) for deployment in Afghanistan. Following the loss of one airframe, this initial capability was augmented in 2012 by five additional airframes, bringing the active fleet to ten. During operations in Afghanistan, RAF Reapers accumulated nearly 54,000 flight hours and executed 459 weapon releases.

As operational requirements shifted to Coalition campaigns against ISIL in Iraq, Syria, and missions over Libya, British Reaper crews flew 2,767 missions and released 896 weapons. However, British airpower doctrine maintains that the Reaper is not an isolated strike mechanism, but rather one complementary tool within a balanced airpower toolkit. Between 2014 and 2017, UK Reaper strikes accounted for 398 out of a total of 1,681 British airstrikes, operating in close coordination with manned fast-jet platforms such as the Tornado GR4 and Typhoon FGR4. To sustain this capability, the Ministry of Defence (MOD) placed orders for up to 16 new airframes with an option for 10 more. Under current procurement frameworks, the existing MQ-9 Reaper fleet is scheduled for complete platform replacement by the technological successor—the "Protector" RG Mk1—a transition originally slated for 2020 but subsequently recalibrated for operational induction in 2024.

##### The United States (USAF / CIA) Context

Conversely, the United States executed a vast expansion of armed RPA operations across the George W. Bush and Barack Obama administrations. Under President Obama, the kinetic deployment of armed drones accelerated exponentially; during his first term alone, President Obama authorized more than six times the number of drone strikes conducted throughout President Bush’s entire eight-year tenure. Furthermore, US strategy fundamentally departed from standard military integration by constructing a dual-track operational architecture: deploying Reapers and Predators through both conventional military channels in the United States Air Force (USAF) and covertly through the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA).

This technological hardware affords unprecedented operational reach, yet the kinetic employment of such systems remains inextricably bound to, and defined by, established international legal frameworks and targeted rules of engagement.

### 2. Rules of Engagement (ROE) and Targeting Frameworks

#### 2.1 The British Operational Framework

The United Kingdom maintains a uncompromising legal doctrine governing the employment of armed RPAs: RAF Reapers operate under the exact same legal, ethical, and Rules of Engagement (ROE) frameworks as manned fighter aircraft. The deployment of kinetic force is strictly governed by International Humanitarian Law (IHL), national legal standards, and established military targeting procedures.

Official statements from RAF operational command confirm that a British Reaper crew is legally permitted to authorize and execute a weapon release under only **three explicit scenario authorizations**:

1. **Imminent Threat to Life:** Where there is a verified belief that friendly coalition forces or civilians are in active contact with enemy forces and facing immediate deadly threat.
2. **Real Evidence of Hostile Intent:** Observed enemy combatants possessing direct weapon systems and actively moving into tactical firing positions in close proximity to Coalition forces.
3. **Witnessing a Hostile Act:** Direct real-time visual tracking of an enemy actively executing a combat strike or hostile action, such as the live planting of an Improvised Explosive Device (IED).

#### 2.2 The American Targeted Killing Framework

The United States engineered a novel targeting architecture that substantially broadened the legal and geographical boundaries of lethal remote force. This framework extended the use of armed drones from standard close air support for troops in contact toward extrajudicial "targeted killing" campaigns.

US strike methodologies are formally divided into two distinct targeting modalities:

- **Personality Strikes:** Lethal kinetic operations directed against specific, pre-identified individuals whose identities have been verified through detailed intelligence dossiers (e.g., high-value commanders within Al-Qaeda or associated forces).
- **Signature Strikes:** Lethal kinetic operations directed against unidentified individuals or groups based entirely on "pattern-of-life" surveillance data, where observed behavioral indicators, locations, and motion vectors are assessed to match the signature of hostile activity.

This operational framework facilitated the geographic expansion of US kinetic strikes far beyond declared warzones (such as active battlefields in Iraq or conventional theaters in Afghanistan) into the sovereign territories of non-declared states, including Pakistan, Yemen, and Somalia. Crucially, by tasking civilian intelligence personnel within the CIA to command and deploy lethal strikes, the US operational model generated intense international legal controversy regarding the erosion of traditional distinctions between combatants and civilian intelligence operatives.

### 3. Crew Dynamics and the Psychological Realities of Remote Warfare

#### 3.1 The Tripartite Ground Control Station (GCS) Crew

Despite being physically removed from the tactical theater of operations, operating an armed MQ-9 Reaper requires an integrated, high-intensity human team inside the Ground Control Station (GCS). This tripartite crew operates in continuous, real-time coordination:

- **The Pilot:** A commissioned officer responsible for the flight control of the aircraft, airspace navigation, flight path management, tactical command, and the ultimate legal and operational authorization to release munitions.
- **The Sensor Operator:** An enlisted specialist tasked with controlling the multi-spectral electro-optical, thermal, and infrared camera suites, maintaining precision target tracking, and guiding laser-guided munitions ("lasing") to target impact.
- **The Intelligence Analyst:** A specialized data analyst embedded directly alongside the flight crew, responsible for real-time intelligence fusion, continuous pattern-of-life analysis, civilian collateral damage estimation, and target verification.

#### 3.2 The "Home Hearth vs. War Front" Paradox

RPA aircrews experience an unprecedented form of combat deployment defined by daily transitions between civilian family existence and high-stakes lethality. Unlike conventional infantry or fast-jet pilots who deploy overseas to physical combat zones, remote crews commute daily to a domestic airbase GCS before returning to their home lives every evening.

This psychological liminality creates a severe spatio-temporal disconnect, as detailed in crew narratives:

> _"He explained to me the odd reality of making his kid’s lunch in the morning, kissing his wife goodbye, then driving to work in traffic, saying hello to his co-workers on base, and then passing through an aluminium door that miraculously transported him to the bloody battlefield of Iraq."_ — (Rogoway, 2015)

> _"Every decision you made was either… somebody living, saving somebody or somebody dying. And you walk into your house and you’re trying to figure out whether your daughter is going to wear a blue tutu or a pink tutu and the disconnection is astounding."_ — (Black, 2013)

This continuous commuting eliminates the traditional military "decompression" period—the vital psychological transition phase provided by returning to a forward operating base or troop transport with peers after combat operations. The requirement to maintain intimate surveillance over targets for weeks or months, followed immediately by domestic domestic routine, generates acute occupational burnout, chronic fatigue, severe psychological distress, and unique manifestations of Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD).

#### 3.3 Cultural Perceptions vs. Military Identity

The military identity of RPA operators is complicated by public, media, and traditional military commentary. Remote crews are regularly subjected to dismissive, pejorative labels that criticize their physical distance from battlefield danger, including:

- _"Cubicle warriors"_
- _"Armchair killers"_
- _"Chair force"_
- _"Button pushers"_

These derogatory caricatures challenge traditional military identity. Historically, the military archetype of the "heroic warrior" is grounded in physical courage, personal bodily vulnerability, and the shared exposure to physical mortality in battle. Because RPA operators face no personal physical risk from enemy fire, external commentators deride their combat role as "riskless" or "deskilled."

However, this critical view fails to capture the complex, hyper-masculinized dynamics of remote operational control. While the elimination of physical bodily risk appears to destabilize the traditional warrior archetype, it simultaneously introduces a demanding regime of technical mastery and "drone stare" omniscience. Operators do not experience a lack of oversight; instead, they function under extreme, relentless surveillance. Live video feeds from their GCS optics are simultaneously broadcast to command headquarters and intelligence centers worldwide. As RAF Reaper operator "Dan" noted in Clark’s research (2019, Endnote 40), the platform is _"the most supervised platform bar none."_ The combination of extreme surveillance intimacy, continuous technical control, and public misperception creates a unique set of operational burdens that conventional models of military identity fail to address.

These operational stressors and identity dynamics within the Ground Control Station are reflected in national debates concerning institutional secrecy and political accountability.

### 4. Public Accountability, Secrecy, and Legal Disquiet

#### 4.1 Comparative Transparency and Reporting Practices

A clear divergence exists within official government communications regarding remote airpower versus manned platforms. While the UK Ministry of Defence (MOD) routinely publishes operational metrics for manned fast-jets—such as releasing detailed strike summaries for Tornado and Typhoon aircraft operating from RAF Akrotiri in Cyprus—it maintains strict operational secrecy regarding its armed RPAs.

The MOD systematically restricts public disclosure regarding:

- The exact operational base locations housing deployed UK Reaper units.
- Specific flight tracking numbers and transit routes.
- Detailed situational context and battle damage assessments for individual Reaper strikes.

This official secrecy creates an information vacuum that fuels public skepticism and parliamentary debate regarding how remote lethal force is governed.

#### 4.2 Political Narrative Management: "Our" Use vs. "Their" Use

To address domestic public disquiet, the British government has consistently attempted to maintain a clear distinction between British RPA deployments and American extrajudicial targeted killing campaigns. Official parliamentary reports explicitly emphasize this distinction:

_"We consider that it is of vital importance that a clear distinction be drawn between the actions of UK Armed Forces operating remotely piloted air systems in Afghanistan and those of other States elsewhere."_ — House of Commons Defence Committee (2014b)

Despite official efforts to frame the Reaper as a conventional airpower asset, this narrative position is challenged by operational realities. British military operations remain linked to US architectures through shared intelligence gathering, joint training pipelines, and embedded RAF personnel flying within American units executing US missions. Crucially, in August 2015, Prime Minister David Cameron announced a targeted UK drone strike in Syria that killed British citizens suspected of plotting terrorist acts. This executive action marked a significant point of policy slippage, where British operational practice crossed from integrated tactical air support into executive targeted killing methodologies, blurring the clear distinction between British and American frameworks.

Synthesizing these strategic, psychological, and institutional factors provides key analytical insights into the operational realities of modern remote warfare.

### 3. Re-Conceptualizing the "Warrior Ethos": Gendered Binaries, Hyper-Masculinity, and Feminization

Military institutions are structurally governed by dominant constructions of military masculinity that equate legitimate combat service with physical deployment, direct bodily risk, and physical valor. When technological innovations disrupt these physical conditions, institutional cultures react by framing remote operators through conflicting gendered lenses. Understanding how technological change disrupts established military identity structures is necessary for preserving force cohesion and personnel management.

**Conceptual Framework: Gendered Constructions of RPAS Operation** Remote warfare challenges traditional military masculinity. Analysts must navigate two competing cultural narratives: the "Feminization Critique" (which derides operators for lack of physical risk) and the "Hyper-Masculine Performance" (which celebrates technological omniscience and the "drone stare"). Utilizing critical theory resolves this apparent contradiction.

#### 3.1 The "Risklessness" Critique & Discourse of Feminization

Because RAF Reaper crews operate thousands of miles from the physical battlefield, external commentators, media outlets, and traditional fast-jet peers frequently apply derogatory labels to RPAS personnel. Terms such as _"cubicle warriors," "armchair killers," "chair force," "Dilbert at war," "button pushers," "warrior geeks,"_ and _"office warriors"_ reflect deep institutional discomfort with disembodied combat.

This terminology stems directly from the perceived "risklessness" of remote operations. In traditional military sociology, the hegemonic "warrior ethos" is defined by personal bodily peril—the willingness to expose oneself to physical harm in combat. Remote operations remove the operator's physical body from direct danger, leading critics to assert that remote warfare erodes classic warrior attributes such as physical courage, chivalry, and bodily valor.

This dynamic illustrates Shannon Vallor’s concept of "moral deskilling." Viewed through a gendered lens, critics argue that automating combat spatiality and removing physical vulnerability deskills the military profession morally and culturally. Because military institutions traditionally code physical risk and bodily dominance as masculine, operating from climate-controlled Ground Control Stations (GCS) leads critics to frame remote warfare as a "feminizing" or degrading influence on armed forces identity.

#### 3.2 Techno-Fetishism, Omniscient Surveillance, and Hyper-Masculinity

Conversely, an opposing narrative frames RPAS operations not as feminizing, but as an expression of techno-fetishism and hyper-masculinity. Within critical security studies, the RPAS platform is conceptualized as a "cyborg soldier"—a fusion of human cognition and advanced technological architecture. The aircraft body, free from human biological limits, physical fatigue, and emotional interference, represents an impenetrable, highly rational data-processing entity that surpasses human physical capacity.

Hyper-masculinity is further performed through the gendered power dynamics of persistent surveillance, termed the "drone stare." Occupying an omniscient "God's-eye view" thousands of feet above the battlefield positions the operator as an empowered, rational watcher. In this visual hierarchy:

- **The Watcher (RPAS Crew):** Coded as objective, dominant, technologically superior, and masculine.
- **The Watched (Ground Subject/Target):** Positioned in a submissive, exposed, highly vulnerable, and feminized role.

Technological proficiency, data processing, and continuous situational awareness are thus re-articulated as modern military virtues. Far from emasculating the operator, technological mastery becomes a high-status performance of military masculinity that projects state power directly into conflict zones.

#### 3.3 Queer Logics and the Breakdown of Gendered Binaries

To reconcile how RPAS crews can be simultaneously derided as "feminized cubicle warriors" and critiqued as "hyper-masculine cyborg operators," defense analysts must move beyond rigid binary models. Applying Cynthia Weber's conceptual framework of "queer logic(s)"—which replaces mutually exclusive A or B binary classifications with an integrative A **and/or** B logic—dismantles this theoretical dichotomy.

Applied to RAF Reaper operations, queer logic demonstrates that remote warfare does not belong exclusively to either category. British Reaper crews simultaneously destabilize and re-inscribe traditional military masculinity:

- They **destabilize** traditional masculinity by eliminating physical bodily peril, operating out of domestic ground stations, and conducting continuous care and surveillance tasks.
- They **re-inscribe** traditional masculinity by executing lethal kinetic strikes, mastering complex weapon systems, protecting ground forces, and projecting state violence.

**Policy Relevance of Critical Gender Theory:** Because crew identity operates beyond a binary (A **and/or** B), traditional defense medical and mental health frameworks that treat remote operators either as front-line fast-jet pilots or as back-office administrative staff fail to diagnose shift-induced operational trauma. Defense policy must recognize this liminal identity to design effective psychological support systems and mitigate force retention crises.

These socio-cultural identity debates shape the daily, lived psychological realities and operational stressors experienced by RPAS personnel.

### 4. The Psychological & Operational Crucible: Domestic Intimacy, Hyper-Surveillance, and Burnout

Strategic defense capabilities depend directly on operator mental health, psychological resilience, and force sustainability. Remote airpower introduces unique spatio-temporal dynamics that challenge traditional operational psychology. Unlike deployed fast-jet crews who transition through structured combat theatres and dedicated post-deployment decompression periods, RPAS operators experience continuous domestic and combat boundary blurring, high supervisory scrutiny, and chronic shift fatigue.

#### 4.1 The Blurred Borderland: Home Hearth vs. War Front

RPAS operators endure a unique form of psychological disorientation caused by daily transitions between quiet domestic life and high-stakes combat operations. Crews commute through local civilian traffic, drop children off at school, pass through the security perimeter of bases like RAF Waddington, and step through a Ground Control Station (GCS) door directly into active combat in Iraq or Syria. Upon completing an eight-hour operational shift involving kinetic strikes or target tracking, operators step back through the same door and commute home to manage routine family decisions.

This psychological dynamic is illustrated by empirical testimonies from operational personnel:

_"He explained to me the odd reality of making his kid’s lunch in the morning, kissing his wife goodbye, then driving to work in traffic, saying hello to his co-workers on base, and then passing through an aluminium door that miraculously transported him to the bloody battlefield of Iraq."_ — **Rogoway (2015)**

_"Every decision you made was either … somebody living, saving somebody or somebody dying. And you walk into your house and you’re trying to figure out whether your daughter is going to wear a blue tutu or a pink tutu and the disconnection is astounding."_ — **Black (2013)**

This spatial intimacy is embodied in the physical layout of operational facilities. At RAF Waddington, a children's playpark is situated directly inside the base perimeter, positioned in close physical proximity to the operational Ground Control Stations where crews conduct lethal strike operations. Operating without physical deployment deprives crews of traditional military transition mechanisms. Standard deployments afford service members geographic separation, shared peer support in theatre, and formal decompression periods before returning home. RPAS crews must process combat trauma and manage domestic responsibilities concurrently, creating continuous boundary-blurring stress.

#### 4.2 Paradoxes of Proximity, Intimacy, and Supervisory Hyper-Visibility

Remote warfare introduces a distinct paradox regarding visual proximity and emotional intimacy. While operators are physically safe thousands of miles away, high-definition optical payloads bring them visually close to targets over extended periods. Crews conduct persistent surveillance on ground subjects for days, weeks, or months, monitoring their daily patterns of life, family interactions, and routines. When authorized to deploy kinetic weapons, crews observe the strike's impact in real-time optical/infrared video, assess post-strike damage, and watch battlefield casualties. This extreme visual intimacy can generate severe psychological distress and unique forms of Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD).

Concurrently, operators face intense institutional scrutiny. Because camera feeds, radio communications, and flight metrics are transmitted in real-time to operational headquarters and senior leadership, RPAS operations are subject to continuous supervisory oversight. As noted in empirical crew feedback:

:::highlight
_"Reaper represents the most supervised platform bar none."_ — **Interview with 'Dan' (RAF Reaper Crew)**
:::

This environment generates a sharp operational paradox: crews are positioned as empowered watchers relative to ground targets, but function as watched, highly disempowered subordinates within their own command structure. Every tactical decision is monitored and recorded, escalating operational stress and reducing tactical autonomy.

#### 4.3 Force Retention, Shift Work, and Chronic Fatigue

These psychological pressures are compounded by operational shift patterns and fatigue. Unlike traditional aircrews who follow structured deployment rotations, Reaper squadrons operate continuous 24/7/365 intelligence and strike lines. Personnel work rotating shift schedules over extended tours, generating chronic circadian disruption and cumulative fatigue.

**Strategic Risk: Operational Strain in RPAS Squadrons** The combination of continuous 24/7/365 shift rotations, high-definition visual trauma, supervisory hyper-visibility, and career stagnation relative to fast-jet pathways creates systemic burnout. This dynamic threatens force retention and increases long-term operational costs for the RAF.

Institutional ambiguity regarding the combat status of RPAS crews worsens this challenge. Stigma within wider defense structures can create perceptions that RPAS service offers fewer career advancement opportunities than traditional manned fast-jet pipelines. This dynamic risks generating retention crises, increasing training costs, and undermining long-term force sustainability.

Translating these empirical insights and sociological frameworks into actionable defense policy reform is essential for sustaining airpower capability.

### I. The Mechanics of "Perpetual War": Executive Elasticity and the Kill List

Howley structures the first section of his critical media analysis around **"Perpetual War" as the dominant order of discourse** [`Howley`, p. xxvi, p. 1]. In his May 23, 2013 address at the National Defense University (NDU), President Barack Obama explicitly acknowledged the systemic trap of this doctrine:

> _"We cannot use force everywhere that a radical ideology takes root; and in the absence of a strategy that reduces the wellspring of extremism, **a perpetual war—through drones or Special Forces or troop deployments—will prove self-defeating, and alter our country in troubling ways**."_ — Barack Obama [`Howley`, p. 60, p. 322]

```txt
  RHETORICAL REBRANDING                          EXECUTIVE REALITY & EXPANSION
  ├── Bush: "Global War on Terror (GWOT)"        ──► Secret "Kill Lists" ("Terror Tuesdays") & "Disposition Matrix"
  │   [Howley, pp. 25–26]                             [Howley, p. xiv, p. 56, p. 312].
  └── Obama: "Overseas Contingency Operations"    ──► 500+ Drone Strikes (10× Bush administration volume)
      [Howley, p. 25, p. 243]                         [Howley, p. 26, p. 45, p. 244].
```

#### 1. Rebranding Imperial Expansion

While the Obama administration attempted to discursively rebrand Bush’s "Global War on Terror" into technocratic euphemisms like _"Overseas Contingency Operations"_ [`Howley`, p. 25, p. 243], Obama executed a massive escalation of remote aerial assassination [`Howley`, p. 26, p. 45]. Between 2009 and 2015, Obama ordered upwards of **500 drone strikes—ten times the number authorized under George W. Bush**—making the Predator and Reaper drones his explicit "weapon of choice" [`Howley`, p. 26, p. 45, pp. 244–246].

#### 2. The Leaked DOJ White Papers & "Elastic" Definitions

The legal foundation for targeted killing rests on secret Department of Justice (DOJ) Office of Legal Counsel (OLC) memoranda and leaked white papers that grant the Executive Branch unchecked power [`Howley`, pp. 52–55, pp. 306–310]. Legal scholars Hina Shamsi and Mary Ellen O'Connell expose how the DOJ concocted "vague, elastic, and robbed of plain meaning" definitions for core legal concepts [`Howley`, p. 54, p. 309]:

- **"Imminent Threat":** Redefined so broadly that the Executive Branch claims authority to assassinate human targets far from any active battlefield without presenting specific evidence of an active, ongoing terror plot [`Howley`, p. 53, p. 307].
- **"Infeasible [Capture]":** Elasticized to mean anything inconvenient, awkward, or politically risky [`Howley`, p. 55, p. 310].
- **Exemption from Judicial Review:** The DOJ memos assert that executive strike decisions—including the extrajudicial execution of US citizens (such as Anwar al-Awlaki, Samir Khan, and 16-year-old Abdulrahman al-Awlaki in Yemen)—are **completely exempt from judicial oversight before or after the fact, anywhere on Earth** [`Howley`, pp. 45–56, 305–308].

### II. Linguistic Engineering and Media Complicity: "Murder Incorporated"

In Chapter 3 (_"Murder Incorporated"_) and Chapter 6 (_"Reporting the Drone Wars"_), Howley details how corporate news organizations operate as de-facto public relations arms for the national security state, constructing an illusory picture of "clean, surgical warfare" [`Howley`, pp. 45, 113–120].

```txt
  DISCURSIVE DECEPTION TECHNIQUE                 SYSTEMIC FUNCTION & IMPACT
  ├── Nominalization ("Drone Strike")             ──► Transforms violent action into an agentless event; erases
  │   [Howley, pp. xxi–xxii, 117]                     human pilots, analysts, & commanders from responsibility [p. 117].
  ├── Over-Lexicalization ("Militant")            ──► Universal catchall label applied to all military-aged males
  │   [Howley, pp. 115–117]                           killed in a strike zone to cover up civilian slaughter [p. 116].
  └── Perception Management & Media Blackout     ──► Complete news blackout of leaked evidence (*"The Drone Papers"*)
      [Howley, pp. 129–130, 186, 467]                 exposing lowball CDE estimates & faulty SIGINT [p. 129].
```

#### 1. Deleting Agency Through Nominalization and Transitivity

Through Norman Fairclough's Critical Discourse Analysis (CDA), Howley demonstrates that headlines routinely use **nominalization**—turning action verbs into abstract nouns [`Howley`, pp. xxi–xxii, 117–118]. The phrase `"US Drone Strike Kills 6"` transforms a violent military execution into a naturalized event, deleting human agency and hiding the 186 operators, analysts, and commanders involved in the kill chain [`Howley`, pp. xxi–xxii, 117–118, 435].

#### 2. The "Militant" Lexical Trap

Glenn Greenwald and linguist Roger Fowler highlight how corporate media uses the word "militant" as an over-lexicalized catchall [`Howley`, pp. 113, 115–116]. Under internal administration rules, **all military-aged males killed within a strike zone are automatically categorized as "combatants" or "militants" posthumously**, unless explicit intelligence emerges proving them innocent after they are dead [`Howley`, p. 116, pp. 249, 720–721].

#### 3. Suppressing "The Drone Papers"

When _The Intercept_ published _The Drone Papers_ in October 2015—leaking classified documents proving that the US drone campaign relied on flawed signals intelligence (SIGINT), lowballed collateral damage estimates (CDE), and resulted in over 90% non-target casualties in certain operations—US corporate media executed a near-total blackout of the story [`Howley`, pp. 129–130, 467–469].

### III. The "Unmanning" Paradox and the Disavowal of Violence

In _Unmanning_, Katherine Chandler analyzes how the drone operates as a performative disavowal of human action [`Chandler`, pp. 2–5; `Howley`, pp. 12–15].

```txt
  PREDATOR STRIKE (February 2002 - Zhawar Kili)   UNMANNING DISAVOWAL MECHANISM
  [ Camera Feed + Hellfire Missile ] ──► [ Closed-Loop Media Unit ] ──► [ "Not Innocent" / Target Erased ]
  - Targeted a "tall man in robes"       - Collapses operator & aircraft      - Executed innocent metal collectors;
    suspected to be Osama bin Laden.       into "machinic unit" [p. 2].         Pentagon claimed strike was "appropriate" [p. 4].
```

1. **The "Different Lethality" Cover:** Following the February 4, 2002 CIA Predator strike in Zhawar Kili, Afghanistan—which murdered innocent local peasants scrounging for scrap metal (Daraz Khan, Jehangir Khan, Mir Ahmed) because a camera saw a "tall man in robes"—Defense Secretary Donald Rumsfeld praised the system's "different lethality" [`Chandler`, pp. 1–4; `Howley`, pp. 33–35]. Pentagon spokesman Rear Adm. Stufflebeem asserted the dead were "not innocents," using the drone camera feed as self-justifying truth [`Chandler`, p. 4; `Howley`, p. 35].
2. **Systemic Disavowal:** Unmanning functions by creating a closed feedback loop between camera, airframe, and missile [`Chandler`, pp. 2, 8]. When a strike succeeds, state power hails "American technological genius"; when it murders civilians or wedding parties (such as the December 2013 Rad'a Yemen wedding strike), the system disavows human political decision-making, framing the massacre as an un-avoidable technical anomaly [`Chandler`, pp. 2, 8; `Howley`, pp. 127–128].

### IV. Innisian Space-Bias, "Mowing the Lawn," and the Whistleblower Witness

In the book's conclusion, Howley applies Harold Innis’s media theory (_Empire and Communications_) to explain why drone warfare leads directly to endless global conflict [`Howley`, pp. 257–265].

```txt
  INNISIAN SPACE-BIASED TECHNOLOGY               OPERATIONAL REALITY OF PERPETUAL WAR
  ├── Annihilates space and geography via        ──► "Mowing the Lawn": Trivializes targeted assassination as a
  │   satellite data links (DCGS) [p. 260].          routine, endless household chore [Howley, p. 265].
  └── "Pernicious neglect of time" & history      └── "Death by Metadata" (SKYNET): Machine-learning algorithms
      [Howley, p. 264; Ccru, p. 30].                 target cell phone SIM cards, not verified humans [p. 268].
```

1. **"Mowing the Lawn":** Military strategists and media commentators use the horrifyingly casual euphemism "mowing the lawn" to describe routine targeted assassination [`Howley`, p. 265]. This domesticating metaphor trivializes state violence, masking a "pernicious neglect of time and history" that ensures permanent regional instability [`Howley`, pp. 264–265].
2. **"Death by Metadata" (SKYNET):** As former NSA/CIA Director Michael Hayden admitted: _"We kill people based on metadata"_ [`Howley`, p. 179, p. 580]. Using machine-learning algorithms like SKYNET, the military targets cell phone SIM cards rather than verified human identities [`Howley`, p. 180, p. 268, p. 758]. Insurgents swap SIM cards in bags, meaning Hellfire missiles routinely annihilate innocent people who happen to be carrying a flagged phone [`Howley`, p. 268, p. 758].
3. **Whistleblower Traumatic Intimacy:** Former drone operators turned whistleblowers (Brandon Bryant, Heather Linebaugh, Cian Westmoreland, Lisa Ling) expose the reality of "distributed intimacy" [`Howley`, pp. 175–186]. Watching human targets for weeks and then viewing their body heat drain onto thermal infrared screens during Battle Damage Assessments (BDA) inflicts severe moral injury [`Howley`, pp. 176–178, 183–185]. Whistleblowers bear witness that drone warfare is not surgical—it is a _"cowardly murder engine that serves as the single greatest recruitment driver for global terrorism"_ [`Howley`, pp. 183–186, 190n3, 598].
