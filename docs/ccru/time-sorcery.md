### 1. Course Foundations & The Meta-Model of Sorcery

This curriculum establishes a rigorous, uncompromising bridge between esoteric theoretical frameworks and practical, results-based sorcery. To systematically evaluate and execute sorcerous operations, magic is structured into a continuous five-stage operational cycle:

1. **Source:** The foundational root of all magical force. Source represents the latent, omnipresent laws or emanations of the universe—whether conceptualized as ley lines, quantum fields, or universal principles. It is a passive, pre-existing condition waiting to be accessed and channeled by an operator.
2. **Imagination:** The primary active step in sorcery. Operating as the highest sense among the eight human senses (sight, smell, touch, taste, sound, mind, memory, and imagination), imagination processes raw sensory data, identifies structural patterns, and formulates concepts of unrealized phenomena. It is the active faculty that allows the practitioner to define, structure, and visualize their explicit will.
3. **Possibility:** The analytical filtering stage. Magical operations travel along the path of highest likelihood. The practitioner calculates the most plausible vectors for realization (e.g., opting for an astral projection or strategic synchronicity over physical teleportation) and selects the appropriate magical machinery—such as specific planetary rites or \#-gram operations—to bridge imagination with material manifestation.
4. **Action:** The operational execution of sorcerous intention. Action encompasses the actual performance of the ritual, spellwork, or sigilization. It demands no rigid ceremonial dogma or vestments; rather, it represents the intentional, precise alignment of non-physical universe forces that yields measurable, physical effects in the material world.
5. **Change:** The materialization of the willed outcome. Change marks the completion of the operational circuit, transforming a previously unmanifested state into an empirical reality. If a spell fails to produce change, the circuit short-circuits, requiring the practitioner to return to the initial stages to re-evaluate vectors, paradigms, and possibilities.
